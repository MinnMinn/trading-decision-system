# Wyckoff ↔ ICT/TTrades concept mapping — file 09

**What this is.** A concept-by-concept correspondence between the classical Wyckoff vocabulary (`knowledge/07`, `knowledge/01`, `knowledge/08`) and the ICT/TTrades vocabulary (`knowledge/04`–`06`), plus a double-counting risk register. `knowledge/10-integrated-method.md` is the operating procedure built on top of this file; this file is the reference it cites.

Purpose: make `docs/architecture/SYSTEM-DESIGN.md` §6.1's Independent-Confluence Check enforceable for the Wyckoff/ICT pair, so the two 0–25 dimensions stop being able to score one phenomenon twice.

**Citation shorthand used throughout.** `k01` = `knowledge/01-footprint-wyckoff-logic-structure-absorption.md`; `k04` = `knowledge/04-ttrades-core-A.md`; `k05` = `knowledge/05-ttrades-core-B.md`; `k06` = `knowledge/06-ttrades-models.md`; `k07` = `knowledge/07-wyckoff-advance.md`; `k08` = `knowledge/08-wyckoff-and-modern-tools.md`. Page citations are the ones the files themselves carry: `WA pNNN` (Wyckoff Advance), `WMT pNNN` (Wyckoff and Modern Tools), `[P1 pN]`/`[P2 pN]`/`[P3 pN]` (the Footprint course PDFs inside k01), and TTrades deck citations as printed in k04–k06 (e.g. `17. Orderblocks p3 (1)`, `Model11 p44`).

## 2. Double-counting risk register (ranked, highest risk first)

Framing: `SYSTEM-DESIGN.md §6.1` already forbids one dimension contributing more than once to the *methodology count*. The rules below are about the **0–25 point allocations inside** the Wyckoff and ICT dimensions, where the same candles can currently earn points twice.

**Risk 1 — Spring ↔ liquidity grab of SSL / turtle soup (and the mirror: UT/UTAD ↔ BSL grab).**
- Why it is one phenomenon: rows 9 and 12 above — both traditions define the identical price event (excursion beyond the boundary, body back inside) from the *same OHLC candles at the same price level*. `k01 §2.8` and `k04 §2.17` are describing one wick.
- What would make them independent: (a) the swept ICT level is a **different** level from the TR boundary — e.g. a PDL/PWL/PML or an equal-lows cluster (`k04 §2.8, §2.7`) materially separated in price from the Wyckoff support; or (b) the Wyckoff side's contribution is restricted to the **volume typing** (Spring type 1/2/3 per `k08 §2.6`) rather than to the existence of the excursion, and real traded volume is available.
- Handling rule: **if the swept level and the Wyckoff TR boundary are the same level (or fall inside the same candle's wick), the excursion scores in exactly ONE dimension.** Recommend: the excursion itself scores in Wyckoff (it is the phase-defining event); the ICT dimension may then score only its *incremental* content — CISD line formed, displacement present, FVG left behind, premium/discount position, killzone alignment — and must state in its output that the sweep is being credited to Wyckoff.

**Risk 2 — SOS/SOW ↔ MSS.**
- Why it is one phenomenon: row 14/16 — one body close beyond one structural level. `k01 §2.8` and `k05 §2.2` differ in *requirements*, not in the event.
- What would make them independent: nothing about the break itself. The only genuinely extra Wyckoff content is (a) the **time** requirement — "a period of commitment above the TR high" (`k01 §2.8`) — and (b) **rising volume** on the break (`k01 §3.4 R18`), which `k04`–`k06` never measure.
- Handling rule: **the structural break scores once.** Score it in ICT (MSS is the more precisely defined test: body close, `k04 §2.17`). The Wyckoff dimension may add points **only** for the acceptance-over-time and effort/volume evidence — and only if it can cite an actual volume series. If the Wyckoff read is "SOS confirmed" with no volume and no dwell-time evidence, it is the MSS restated: score 0 there.

**Risk 3 — LPS/BU ↔ OB / breaker / FVG / mitigation / OTE / Unicorn (the retest pile-up).**
- Why it is one phenomenon: row 18 — one pullback. Worse, the ICT side can self-multiply: the Unicorn is *defined* as breaker ∩ FVG at one price (`k05 §2.8`, `k06 §2.11`), and `k05 §2.5` shows OB+FVG confluence at one price too.
- What would make them independent: different pullbacks (a first LPS and a later LPS at a different price and time), or an ICT array on a different timeframe from the Wyckoff LPS.
- Handling rule: **one pullback ⇒ at most one ICT location observation and at most one Wyckoff LPS observation, and if they are the same pullback, only one of the two scores.** An OB+FVG+breaker stack at one price is a **quality grade** of a single location (score it higher inside the 0–10 location bucket), never as three confluences. Recommend the ICT dimension owns the location (it has the sub-levels), the Wyckoff dimension owns "supply declining into the pullback" (`k01 §3.4 R19`) only if volume evidence exists.

**Risk 4 — Wyckoff TR boundary ↔ ICT BSL/SSL line.**
- Why it is one phenomenon: rows 28–30 — the same horizontal line reached by two derivations (event-derived vs swing-derived).
- What would make them independent: the ICT liquidity level is a **calendar-derived** reference (PDH/PDL, PWH/PWL, PMH/PML — `k04 §2.8`) rather than the range extreme, and sits at a materially different price.
- Handling rule: **before scoring, the structure read must print both numbers — the Wyckoff boundary price and the ICT liquidity price.** If they round to the same level, "price is at the TR low" and "price is at SSL" are one observation. If they differ by more than the instrument's typical wick, they are two, and both may score.

**Risk 5 — Wyckoff dealing-range read ↔ ICT premium/discount positioning.**
- Why it is one phenomenon: row 48 — if the ICT range is anchored on the same two extremes as the Wyckoff TR, then "price is in discount" contains no information beyond "price is in the lower half of the TR", which the Wyckoff phase read already implies.
- What would make them independent: the premium/discount range is anchored on a **different** dealing range (different timeframe, or a swing range that is not the TR), or the comparison is against **VAH/VAL** (`k08 §5 Step 4`), which is volume-weighted and can disagree with the geometric EQ.
- Handling rule: **premium/discount scores only when its anchors are stated and differ from the Wyckoff TR anchors.** Otherwise score 0 for that sub-item and say so.

**Risk 6 — Phase C ↔ PO3 "Manipulation".**
- Why it is one phenomenon: row 25/51 — when the PO3 period and the TR coincide in timeframe, the manipulation leg *is* the Phase C test.
- What would make them independent: PO3 is scoped to a single period's open (`k05 §2.11`); if the PO3 unit (e.g. the daily candle) is a genuinely different scale from the TR (e.g. a multi-week range), the two observations refer to different swings.
- Handling rule: **require the PO3 period unit to be named.** If the PO3 manipulation leg is the same swing as the Phase C excursion, it scores once — and it is already covered by Risk 1's rule.

**Risk 7 — Phase D ↔ PO3 "Distribution" leg / "candle 3 expansion".**
- Why it is one phenomenon: row 26 — the same expansion leg. Compounded by the **vocabulary inversion**: PO3's "distribution" is the markup leg, Wyckoff's "distribution" is topping.
- Handling rule: **ban the bare token "distribution" from cross-dimension reasoning.** Require the qualified form ("Wyckoff distribution phase" vs "PO3 distribution leg") in every output line. Then apply Risk 2's rule — the expansion leg scores once.

**Risk 8 — Wyckoff "widening spread" ↔ ICT "displacement".**
- Why it is one phenomenon: row 42 — literally the same measurement (candle body/range size) on the same candle.
- What would make them independent: the **volume** attached to that candle, which only Wyckoff reads (`k01 §4.1`).
- Handling rule: **spread-widening is not separate evidence from displacement.** If the ICT dimension has scored displacement, the Wyckoff dimension may cite only the volume attached to it, and only if the volume source is real traded size. Under the MT5 tick-volume caveat (`SYSTEM-DESIGN.md §3`, and step 4 of the current `wyckoff-skill/SKILL.md`), that Wyckoff credit must be reduced, not taken at face value.

**Risk 9 — Spring type 3 ("Shake Out", high volume) ↔ bearish MSS. This one is a CONTRADICTION masquerading as confluence.**
- Why it matters: `k08 §2.6` types a high-volume break that "may keep falling briefly" as **Spring type 3 — bullish**. `k01 §4.3` defines a **Shakeout** as most candles *closing* below the boundary. But `k04 §2.17` classifies body closes beyond structure as **displacement/MSS — bearish**, and `k04 §3.6 R25` says a later body close beyond the level **converts a grab into an MSS**. The same candles are therefore a bullish Wyckoff event and a bearish ICT event.
- Handling rule: **when a Wyckoff Spring type 3 / Shakeout coincides with body closes beyond the boundary, the scorer must raise a Contradiction (`SYSTEM-DESIGN.md §6.2`, opposing HTF structure penalty), never award both dimensions.** No source file resolves this — see §5.

**Risk 10 — "CHOCH" scored in the ICT dimension.**
- Why it is one phenomenon: rows 22 and 40 — the ICT corpus contains no CHOCH. Any CHOCH claim is either (a) folklore with no source, or (b) the Wyckoff ChoCh (`k01 §2.5`) restated, in which case it is a pure double-count of the Wyckoff read.
- Handling rule: **reject "CHOCH" as an ICT-dimension observation outright.** If the observation is real, cite `k01 §2.5` and score it in Wyckoff. Same treatment for "BOS" (row 39) unless explicitly mapped to BMS with a `k01 §4.7` citation.

**Risk 11 — Wyckoff SC ↔ ICT "ERL taken".**
- Why it is one phenomenon: row 3 — the same low. `k05 §2.13`'s ERL is the swing low; `k01 §2.8`'s SC is the climax low. On a range bottom these are usually the same candle.
- Handling rule: same as Risk 4 — print both prices; if identical, one observation. The Wyckoff-only content is the **volume climax** (`k01 §4.2`: "S>>, D>>"), which may be scored separately if a real volume series backs it.

**Risk 12 — "Local accumulation as Spring" ↔ TTrades Fractal Model LTF confirmation.**
- Why it is one phenomenon: row 54 — both are *the same zoom on the same candles*. `k01 §4.11` says the HTF Spring contains an LTF PS–SC–AR–ST; `k06 §2.3` says the HTF candle-2 swing point is confirmed by an LTF CISD within candle 2. Running both and counting two confirmations counts one zoom twice.
- Handling rule: **the multi-timeframe decomposition of a single HTF event scores once.** Pick one vocabulary for the LTF read per analysis run and record which. The legitimate stacking case is different (§3 item 8): an HTF *phase* call plus an LTF *trigger* on genuinely different swings.

1. **SMT / cross-asset divergence** (`k05 §2.10, §3.2 R9`) vs any single-chart Wyckoff read. Different **data source** — a second instrument's price series. Caveat: `k04 §6 items 1–3` leave the applicable killzone set and DST handling unresolved for crypto/commodities, so this stack is real but its *parameters* are unsourced.

Legitimately stacks — **conditional on the volume being real traded size.** Under MT5 tick-volume (`SYSTEM-DESIGN.md §3`) this independence largely collapses and the Wyckoff credit must be reduced. Note also that Risk 8 applies: the *spread* half of the Wyckoff read is not independent, only the volume half is.
4. **Calendar-derived HTF liquidity levels** — PDH/PDL, PWH/PWL, PMH/PML (`k04 §2.8`; `k01 §2.7 [P2 p24, p29]`) — when they sit at a price materially different from the Wyckoff TR boundary. 5. **Two independent targeting methods agreeing.** Wyckoff's Cause-and-Effect P&F horizontal count (`k01 §2.1 [P1 p4]`, three counts) and the "opposite side of the TR / Market-Profile 80% rule" (`k08 §5 Step 6, WMT p273`) vs ICT's standard-deviation projections of the manipulation leg at −2/−2.5/−4 (`k05 §2.12, §3.4 R16–R19`). ## 4. Sequencing / precedence

### Recommended order

1. **Regime + Trading Range existence + phase — Wyckoff first.** `k04`–`k06` contain **no** phase, campaign, or cause vocabulary; the longest-horizon narrative ICT offers is a weekly profile (`k06 §2.5.4`) or a previous-day bias (`k04 §2.12`). `k08 §5 Step 1 (WMT p233–p236)` prescribes exactly this order — zoom out for the broader trend, then find the local non-trending zone. Carry a confidence: `k08 §2.5 (WMT p236)` says reaccumulation-vs-distribution is one of the hardest calls in the method.
2. **Which event to expect next (Spring vs Upthrust, and its type) — Wyckoff.** `k08 §5 Step 2 (WMT p236–p238)`: accumulation/reaccumulation → look for a Spring type at the range low; distribution/redistribution → an Upthrust type at the range high. This produces the *hypothesis* the rest of the pipeline tests.
3. **HTF structural falsification — ICT, used as a check, not a tiebreak.** `k05 §2.2, §3.1 R1`: "It is important that a higher time frame level is engaged prior to the lower time frame market structure shift." If the last HTF body-close break contradicts the phase call from step 1, that is a **Contradiction** under `SYSTEM-DESIGN.md §6.2` — it must not be resolved by quietly preferring one tradition.
4. **Entry location — ICT leads, Wyckoff filters.** ICT's location vocabulary is far more resolved: OB open and mean threshold 0.5 (`k05 §2.5`), FVG edges plus consequent encroachment (`k04 §2.23, §2.26`), breaker box (`k05 §2.7`), OTE 0.62–0.79 (`k04 §2.20`). Wyckoff's location vocabulary is "at the Spring low / at the LPS" with no sub-level (`k01 §3.7 R37`).
5. **Location veto — Wyckoff/Volume-Profile.** `k08 §5 Step 4 (WMT p243–p249)` is an explicit abandon rule: if price crosses cleanly through VAH/VAL into the LVN **without a reversal reaction**, abandon the Spring/Upthrust plan — the market is trending, not ranging. This is the strongest thesis-invalidation statement in either tradition and has no ICT counterpart. Run it *after* location selection, before timing.
6. **Timing — ICT, essentially uncontested.** Killzone/session (`k04 §2.1`), daily profile London-vs-New-York reversal (`k06 §2.5.6`), and the CISD trigger (`k05 §2.3`; `k06 §2.5.7`: "wait for an intraday CISD to be established before attempting to get onside"). Important nuance for the skill rewrite: `k08`'s own timing trigger at `§5 Step 5` is a **Footprint/Delta** read (exhaustion → absorption → development), not a Wyckoff-structure read — so the timing contest is really *ICT vs Footprint*, not Wyckoff vs ICT.
7. **Invalidation — must be chosen explicitly, because the traditions disagree (below).**

### Where the two traditions disagree about invalidation

| | Stop / invalidation | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| **Wyckoff** | Below the **lowest low of the Spring** (above the highest high of the Upthrust) — placed so the stop only triggers on genuine pattern failure. Plus the thesis-level abandon rule at VAL/VAH→LVN. | `k08 §5 Step 6 (WMT p271)`; `k08 §5 Step 4 (WMT p243–p249)`; `k01 §3.7 R37` |
| **ICT** | Three nested price choices: FVG far edge (tight) / OB candle low (medium) / originating swing low (wide). Plus a *soft* warning at CE — a body close through the FVG's 0.5. Plus TTrades' own rule: stop at the **candle-2 swing point**. | `k04 §3.6 R22, R23`; `k05 §3.5 R21, R22`; `k06 §2.5.7 (Model11 p90–p92)` |

**The disagreement is material, not cosmetic.** ICT's tight and medium options (FVG far edge, OB low, breaker box low) sit **inside** the swept extreme — i.e. inside the Wyckoff stop. Only ICT's *wide* option ("below the raid swing low", `k05 §3.3 R10`) coincides with Wyckoff's. The same setup can therefore carry stops that differ by the entire depth of the sweep, which changes position size by the same factor.

Recommendations (the main session decides the rubric; these are the constraints the sources impose):
- **Name one invalidation owner per trade plan and record it.** Sizing and management must both use that one level. Sizing off the tight ICT stop while managing to the wide Wyckoff stop is silent risk inflation.
- ICT carries a **thesis** invalidation with no Wyckoff analogue: `k04 §3.6 R25` — a later body close beyond the swept level converts the grab into an MSS, i.e. the reversal read is dead even if the stop has not been hit. Worth surfacing as a separate invalidation channel alongside `k08 §5 Step 4`'s LVN rule.
- A second, quieter disagreement: **ICT says buy in discount** (`k04 §2.19`: longs in discount give RR>1, longs in premium RR<1), but Wyckoff's *best* long add-on is the LPS in Phase D (`k01 §3.4 R19`), which forms after the SOS and is therefore frequently in **premium** by ICT's own measure. The two traditions are not describing the same trade at that point — Wyckoff is describing a trend add, ICT a range entry — and a scorer that penalises "long in premium" will systematically down-score valid Phase-D adds.
