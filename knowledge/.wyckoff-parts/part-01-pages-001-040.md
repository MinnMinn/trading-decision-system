### 3.2 Three Laws (continued) — Cause & Effect, Effort vs Result
- `[text]` **p016 (printed p17):**
  - **Quy luật nguyên nhân – kết quả (Law of Cause and Effect):** "Highlights the relationship between cause (which is preparation) and effect (which is the price move). Cause can be understood as the accumulation or distribution of an asset over a given period of time. Effect is the large price move that occurs afterward. For example, a long accumulation phase can be the cause of a strong uptrend, while a long distribution phase can lead to a deep decline." — `p016`
  - **Quy luật nỗ lực – kết quả (Law of Effort vs Result):** "Concerns the correlation between trading volume (effort) and price movement (result). If a large price move (result) is supported by high trading volume (great effort), this shows a significant change in the supply-demand balance and increases the validity of the price action. Conversely, if the price move is not supported by corresponding trading volume, this points to weakness or lack of sustainability in the trend." — `p016`
  - The author states the three laws will be interwoven and explained in detail across later chapters' analyses; he considers them the analytical framework underlying his trading decisions. — `p016`
- `[text]` **Section "3. Chu kỳ giá Wyckoff (Wyckoff Price Cycle)", p016:** "The Wyckoff Price Cycle is a description of a repeating process in which the market moves through the phases of accumulation, uptrend (Markup), distribution (Distribution), and downtrend (Markdown). This cycle reflects the view that the market operates based on the preparation and execution of price campaigns by the 'strong hands' (large institutional investors and professional investors)." — `p016`

- `[text]` **p017 continued:** "In the price cycle, alongside the 'strong hands,' there are also 'weak hands' — retail investors lacking knowledge. Usually when the 'weak hands' are afraid and sell off or leave the market, that is when the 'strong hands' are accumulating stocks or assets. Conversely, when the 'weak hands' feel excited and confident in the market, that is when the strong hands are distributing." "This is precisely the reason 99% of investors in financial markets lose money. Most of us fall into **FOMO** (**Fear Of Missing Out**), get swept up by strong market moves, are drawn in by good news, and easily fall into a state of panic due to weak psychology and limited knowledge. Therefore, small retail investors often buy tops and sell bottoms, unable to recognize the recurring trap of the price cycle." — `p017`

### 3.4 Strong-hand / weak-hand behaviour by stage
- `[text]` **p018 (printed p19):** "Below is the difference in behavior between 'weak hands' and 'strong hands' across the stages of the price cycle." — `p018`
  - **Giai đoạn 1 (Stage 1):** "When the market falls sharply, 'weak hands' sell off in panic and lose confidence, while strong hands accumulate cheap goods from the weak hands." — `p018`
  - **Giai đoạn 2 (Stage 2):** "When price starts to rise, 'strong hands' recognize the uptrend and continue to buy, while 'weak hands' hesitate or sell off whatever remains." Near the end of the uptrend, good news appears, and many influential people make money from the market, causing 'weak hands' to fear missing the opportunity and buy regardless of price; 'strong hands' observe weak-hand behavior and prepare a distribution plan. — `p018`
  - **Giai đoạn 3 (Stage 3):** "Price rises high, 'strong hands' begin a planned distribution, without causing excessive price disturbance, in order to maintain 'weak hands' confidence in the continued rise. 'Weak hands' buying demand helps 'strong hands' offload a large amount of goods at good prices." — `p018`
  - **Giai đoạn 4 (Stage 4):** "When price falls and forms a downtrend, 'strong hands' sell as soon as they notice early signs of the downtrend. 'Weak hands' still believe in the market and buy in, thinking the decline is just a consolidation before rising again." — `p018`
  - "As such, we can clearly see the reason so many people lose money when participating in financial markets." — `p018`

### 3.6 SOT detail: recognition, downtrend/uptrend variants with volume
- `[text]` **p020 (printed p21):** "Recognizing SOT is fairly simple: we just need to observe that a new stopping point (peak/trough) has a narrower range/amplitude than the previous stopping point, proving that price momentum has decreased across three attempted pushes; notably, failure on the third push provides an early reversal warning signal to the investor." ### 3.7 Trading Range (definition and fractality)
- `[text]` **Section "5. Phạm vi giao dịch (Trading Range)", p023 (printed p24):** "Trading Range, also called the trading range/box, is the zone in which an asset's price fluctuates within a certain price band but does not break through significant support or resistance levels over a relatively long period of time." "I will usually refer to the Trading Range informally as 'the box' ('cái hộp') or the 'sideways price zone' ('khu vực giá đi ngang'). I will use these three terms interchangeably in the book. There are many different types of Trading Range in the market, but I only focus on zones where price is moving sideways and forming relatively level highs and lows. Put simply, price forms a rectangle shape." — `p023`
- `[text]` "Thus, within a Trading Range, price moves up and down between two specific price levels: the support level (the low price at which the asset tends to see buying strong enough to prevent price from falling further) and the resistance level (the high price at which the asset faces selling pressure large enough to prevent price from rising further). While the asset trades within this range, it shows a temporary balance between buyers and sellers, with neither side having a clear advantage to create a new trend (Hình 1.6 and Hình 1.7)." — `p023`
- `[diagram]` **Hình 1.6: "Trading Range của ARB/USDT khung 30 phút" (Trading Range of ARB/USDT, 30-min), p024.** A candlestick price chart annotated with horizontal support/resistance bands (colored blue/orange highlight bars) marking two successive, progressively lower trading ranges over time, each with a midline dotted line connecting range midpoints. — `p024`
- `[diagram]` **Hình 1.7: "Trading Range của cổ phiếu GMD khung 240 phút" (Trading Range of GMD stock, 240-min/H4), p024.** A candlestick chart of Vietnamese stock GMD showing a sequence of stacked/rising trading ranges (each a horizontal band) with dotted lines connecting range midpoints, illustrating successive ranges within an uptrend. — `p024`
- `[text]` **p025 (printed p26):** "When observing a Trading Range in the market, an important point to note is that the market has the property of 'Fractality,' meaning patterns and behaviors can repeat across many different time frames. Within a large Trading Range, the reader can find many smaller Trading Ranges. These smaller Trading Ranges can be identified more clearly when zoomed in and observed on lower time frames. For example, while you might see one Trading Range on a daily chart, you could also find many smaller Trading Ranges on an hourly or minute chart of the same asset. Each smaller Trading Range can offer opportunities and important information for intraday or even shorter-term trades. Understanding the market's 'fractality' helps you realize that the rules and behavior within a large Trading Range are similar to those within smaller Trading Ranges." — `p025`

### 3.8 Market phases (Wyckoff's four Trading-Range types) and the five-step structure
- `[text]` **Section "6. Các giai đoạn thị trường" (Market phases), p025:** "As analyzed above, the Trading Range gives a visual view of the balance between supply and demand over a given period, where price does not swing too widely between support and resistance levels. However, to better understand market behavior, precisely identify the current phase, and predict the next direction, Wyckoff classified Trading Ranges into four main groups (also called market phases/stages): accumulation ('tích lũy'), distribution ('phân phối'), reaccumulation ('tái tích lũy'), and redistribution ('tái phân phối')." — `p025`
- `[text]` **p026 (printed p27):** "Within each phase there are five important steps: stopping the prior trend ('Dừng xu hướng trước'), accumulating the cause ('Tích lũy nguyên nhân'), testing the opposing side ('Đánh giá phe đối lập'), initiating the [new] trend ('Khởi phát xu hướng'), and confirming the trend ('Xác nhận xu hướng') (Hình 1.8)." The author states that in this book he will **not** apply the Wyckoff-Advance-style event labels (PS, SC, AR, ST, UT, UTAD, etc.) to each step; instead he will only describe each step generally, its purpose, and note the important positions within each phase that are analyzed further in later chapters. — `p026`
- `[diagram]` **Hình 1.8: "Các giai đoạn thị trường" (Market phases), p026.** A swing-price schematic divided into five vertical zones labeled (left to right): "1. Dừng xu hướng trước đó" (1. Stop the prior trend), "2. Tích lũy nguyên nhân" (2. Accumulate the cause), "3. Đánh giá phe đối lập" (3. Test the opposing side), "4. Khởi phát xu hướng" (4. Initiate the trend), "5. Xác nhận xu hướng" (5. Confirm the trend). The price path starts high, falls sharply (zone 1), then oscillates sideways within a horizontal band with upper/lower boundary lines and a dotted midline (zones 2–3, showing a slight downward-sloping upper boundary), before breaking out and rallying upward through zones 4–5. — `p026`

### 3.9 Accumulation (6.1)
- `[text]` **Section "6.1. Tích lũy (Accumulation)", p027 (printed p28):** "Accumulation is when large investors begin buying into an asset without causing a sudden price rise. This usually happens after a long price-decline phase or during a phase where price moves sideways but does not fall further (Hình 1.9)." "The goal of the accumulation phase is to accumulate as much of the asset as possible (stocks, commodities, digital currencies...) before starting a new uptrend." — `p027`
- `[diagram]` **"Sơ đồ Tích Lũy #1: Giai đoạn và sự kiện Wyckoff" and "#2" (Accumulation Schema #1 and #2, Hình 1.9), p027.** Two side-by-side Wyckoff accumulation schematics (labeled sourced from "Wyckoff Advance" per the caption on the next page), each divided into Phase A through Phase E (dashed vertical lines), with classic Wyckoff-schematic event labels: PS (Preliminary Support), SC (Selling Climax), AR (Automatic Rally), ST (Secondary Test, also "ST in Phase B"), SPRING, Test, SOS (Sign of Strength), BU/LPS (Back-Up / Last Point of Support), LPS, and horizontal "Vùng kháng cự" (Resistance zone) / "Vùng hỗ trợ" (Support zone) bands. Schema #2 is a simplified variant of #1 with the same phase/event labels. — `p027`
- `[text]` **Caption, p027 (printed p28):** "Hình 1.9: Giai đoạn tích lũy (Nguồn tham khảo: Wyckoff Advance)" — i.e. explicitly credited by the author as sourced from his other book "Wyckoff Advance," not original to this chapter's diagrams. — `p027`
- `[text]` **p028 (printed p29) — "Các bước trong giai đoạn tích lũy" (Steps within the accumulation phase):**
  - "Dừng xu hướng trước đó (Stop the prior trend): The downtrend stops, usually shown through a sudden increase in volume and strong price volatility." — `p028`
  - "Tích lũy nguyên nhân (Accumulate the cause): Price fluctuates within a narrow range; large investors accumulate the asset." — `p028`
  - "Đánh giá phe đối lập (Test the opposing side): Large investors test the market's readiness to begin a new trend. This is shown by pushing price below the support of the Trading Range to test the remaining selling pressure and determine whether there is enough support to prevent price from falling further (Spring). The Spring position is an important position that must be examined in the accumulation phase." — `p028`
  - "Khởi phát xu hướng (Initiate the trend): A new trend begins with a price rise accompanied by large volume that typically breaks through resistance." — `p028`
  - "Xác nhận xu hướng (Confirm the trend): The new uptrend is confirmed, price rises strongly and breaks through important resistance levels." — `p028`

### 3.10 Distribution (6.2)
- `[text]` **Section "6.2. Phân phối (Distribution)", p028:** "The distribution phase usually occurs after a period of continuous price increase and is when large investors start gradually selling off assets. Like accumulation, distribution also occurs without causing an immediately significant price decline." — `p028`
- `[text]` **p029 (printed p31, continuing) — purpose and steps:** "The purpose of distribution is to reduce the amount of assets held by large investors before price starts falling sharply, thereby preserving the profit earned from the prior price-increase phase." Steps: "Dừng xu hướng trước đó: The uptrend stops, usually signaled by a peak in volume and price volatility." "Tích lũy nguyên nhân: Price still fluctuates within a range but does not rise further." "Đánh giá phe đối lập: Large investors test the market's readiness to begin a new downtrend. This is shown by pushing price up above the resistance of the Trading Range to test buying pressure and determine whether there is enough selling force to push price down, via one or more Upthrusts. This position plays an important role and must be examined in the distribution phase." "Khởi phát xu hướng: A new downtrend begins, price falls sharply under sellers' control." "Xác nhận xu hướng: The downtrend is confirmed when price falls sharply below key support levels." — `p029, p030`
