## 2. Core concepts (as the source defines them)

### 2.1 Foundational laws / theories

**Four important theories** `[P1 p2]`: (1) Supply–Demand, (2) Cause and Effect, (3) Effort and Result, (4) Auction. The deck's slide title says "3 Wyckoff laws" for the first three; Auction theory is the fourth and is expanded in PDF 2.

- **Law of Supply and Demand** `[P1 p3]` — "the basic law that auction theory relies on; therefore supply–demand governs every change in price." If demand > supply → price rises. If supply > demand → price falls. If supply = demand → price does not change. (Slide chart labels: "Giá bán" = price, "Khối lượng" = volume.)
- **Law of Cause and Effect** `[P1 p4]` — "an action does not happen by itself; to see a change in price, the root cause must be built first." The result that pushes price up is built from the cause: supply released to the market being **absorbed** to push price up. The effect produced by a cause is always **proportional** to that cause: a large cause → a larger target; a small cause → a lower target. Illustrated with a VNINDEX 15m **Point & Figure** chart (Traditional, box 0.5, reversal 1) showing box counts `[124] box`, `[36] box`, `[76] box` and three targets labelled *Conservative Count*, *Probable Count*, *Aggressive Count* with price objectives 1208, 1205, 1166.
- **Law of Effort and Result** `[P1 p5]` — In financial markets **effort = volume, result = price**. Price behaviour must be reflected by volume, because without effort price cannot move sustainably. "Price is not the most important factor in the market — volume is — but both are the foundation of the Wyckoff method." The sign of participation of large operators is determined by an **increase in volume**.
  - **Harmony (Hài hòa)** vs **Divergence (Phân kỳ)** `[P1 p5–8]` (see §4.1 for the full matrix).

- **Auction Theory** `[P2 p15]` — "Facilitates price negotiation." **Efficient time**: where buyers and sellers comfortably negotiate. **Inefficient time**: where one of the two sides takes control and creates a trend. Cycle diagram: *Comfortable auction* → (Supply exceeds Demand / Demand exceeds Supply) → *Imbalance occurs* → *Search for a balance zone* → back to comfortable auction. `[P2 p18]` Balance → Imbalance → Balance → Imbalance → Balance (each imbalance leg connects two balance boxes).
- **Value** `[P2 p19]` — "The auction process in financial markets is based on value, and value consists of: **Price + Time + Volume = Value**."
- **Acceptance / Rejection** `[P2 p21]` — Acceptance of price into a new area is recognised by price **staying there for a period of time** while buyers + sellers begin negotiating, shown as a sideways area. Rejection: a **quick return to the old value zone**. A balance zone ends when there is no longer consensus on price between buyers and sellers → value changes. An imbalance zone ends when price develops to where buyers and sellers again agree on value. "This is the continuous cycle of the market."

### 2.2 Composite Man / Composite Operator (CO)

`[P1 p9]` Wyckoff proposed a method to study the behaviour of market makers on each product or the whole market; he called them the **Composite Operator**. Theory: these are the people behind price manipulation, to your disadvantage if you do not understand their game, and a source of large profit if you do. He advises retail traders to understand and play the game the CO plays. Wyckoff's observations: the CO carefully **plans, executes and concludes** his campaigns; the CO **attracts the public to buy** a product he has accumulated in large quantity through many transactions; one must study individual charts to determine the product's behaviour and the motives of the CO controlling it; with study and practice one can interpret the motives behind the action the chart describes and identify opportunities early enough to profit. Elsewhere the decks use "CO" and "tay mạnh" (strong hands) interchangeably.

### 2.3 Price cycle

- `[P1 p10]` In **accumulation** the CO buys all product available; when they determine little outside supply remains, they start the **uptrend**. The trend phase is "the path of least resistance." In **distribution** the CO completes distributing (selling) what was bought earlier; the last greedy buyers participate and operators' sell entries are released **together with good news**. A cycle is complete when all phases are observed: **accumulation → uptrend → distribution → downtrend**. Complete cycles occur in **all timeframes**.
- **Wyckoff Logic schematic — 4 market phases** `[P1 p10, p13]`: 1. Accumulation (Tích lũy), 2. Markup (Đánh dấu tăng), 3. Distribution (Phân phối), 4. Markdown (Đánh dấu giảm). Annotated events on the schematic: *Selling climax* → *Spring* → *Test* → *Low-volume failed sell-off* → Accumulation "(Cause)" → *Low-volume sell-off* → *Jump across the creek* → *Markup effect* → *Buying climax* → Distribution "(Cause)" → *Failed rally* → *Test* → *Low-volume rally* → *Ice break (Phá băng)* → *Markdown effect* → "BREAK WITH VOLUME".
- **Auction-theory price cycle (4 steps)** `[P2 p3, p20]`: 1. Start Accumulation Phase (range) → 2. Fast Price Move → 3. Building Pre-Tension (a smaller range mid-trend) → 4. Trend Continuation; the same four steps are drawn for the top (distribution) and each subsequent leg.
- **3 market states** `[P2 p2]`: Consolidation (Củng cố) ≈ **70%** of the time; Downtrend + Uptrend ≈ **30%**.
- Real examples: VNINDEX 1D 2018–2023 `[P1 p11, P2 p4]` — ranges stacked; "Urgent Demand" marked at the March-2020 low with a volume-surge circle; "Selling" marked at the late-2021/early-2022 top with a volume circle; a third volume circle at mid-2023. VIC weekly 2008–2024 `[P1 p12, P2 p5]` — "VIC grew >> 50x in 12 years"; volume annotations `S<<, D>>` in the 2013–2016 base, `S>>, D<<` in the 2021–22 top, `S>>, D>>>` in 2023 and a `Shakeout [C]` at the 2023 low.

### 2.4 Strong hands vs weak hands

- `[P1 p14]` **Strong hands (Tay mạnh)**: institutions or investors who trade based on the **harmonious** movement of price in the cycle. **Weak hands (Tay yếu)**: some investors who trade based on the **disharmony** of price movement in the cycle. Diagram labels — Accumulation zone: "extreme point of fear and capitulation by weak hands"; "first sign of smart accumulation by strong hands"; "selling price"; "accumulation by institutions that accept the trend early"; "selling, short-term trading, or inactivity by weak hands". Distribution zone: "first sign of smart distribution by strong hands"; "point of extreme excitement by weak hands"; "buying price"; "value buying by weak hands"; "distribution on the way down by strong hands".
- `[P1 p15]` **Institutional (strong-hand) behaviour**: buy when price falls (accumulation zone) → buy during the uptrend → inactive → sell when price rises (distribution zone) → sell into capitulation (downtrend). **Weak-hand behaviour**: sell by capitulation (downtrend) → sell when price rises (accumulation zone) → buy during the uptrend → inactive → buy when price falls (distribution zone).
- `[P1 p16]` **13-point cycle map** (three actor classes: Weak hands = institutions + retail [red]; Strong hands = value institutions [blue]; Strong hands = trend-following institutions [yellow]). Each numbered point is a Phase A→Phase D box with a CHoCH label:
  1. Weak hands sell / Value institutions buy the lower range / Trend institutions inactive
  2. Weak hands inactive / Value institutions buy here / Trend institutions buy
  3. Weak hands buy / Value institutions take profit / Trend institutions buy
  4. Weak hands inactive / Value institutions buy part at the bottom / Trend institutions buy
  5. Weak hands buy / Value institutions take profit / Trend institutions buy
  6. Weak hands inactive / Value institutions buy part at the bottom / Trend institutions buy
  7. Weak hands buy / Value institutions sell (Distribution) / Trend institutions buy
  8. Weak hands buy / Value institutions inactive / Trend institutions sell (Distribution)
  9. Weak hands buy / Value institutions inactive / Trend institutions sell (short-term)
  10. Weak hands buy / Value institutions inactive / Trend institutions inactive
  11. Weak hands buy / Value institutions inactive / Trend institutions sell (short-term)
  12. Weak hands buy / Value institutions inactive / Trend institutions inactive
  13. Weak hands sell / Value institutions buy the lower range / Trend institutions inactive

### 2.5 Change of Behaviour (ChoBev) and Change of Character (ChoCh)

- **ChoBev — Thay đổi hành vi** `[P1 p17]`: "The first sign of price and volume behaviour that differs from the current behaviour." Characteristics: (a) a rising reaction against the current trend; (b) increasing spread against the current trend; (c) increasing effort on the reversal reaction. On charts `[P1 p19, p22]` the bearish ChoBev checklist is: "1. Largest down-reaction within the uptrend; 2. Increased spread; 3. Supply increases."
- **ChoCh — Thay đổi đặc tính** `[P1 p17]`: "A series of behaviour changes that leads to a change in price structure." Characteristics: (a) the largest reaction against the current trend; (b) followed by non-continuation of the current trend. Schematic: CHoCH boxes sit at Phase A (PS/SC/AR/ST or PSY/BC/AR/ST) and at Phase C–D (Spring/SOS/BU-LPS or UTAD/LPSY/SOW). Timeline: "Trend environment unchanged → Trend environment changed → Trend environment unchanged → Trend."
- Chart usage `[P1 p18, p21]`: "Change of character: from downtrend to sideways" (at the first higher low after a selling climax) and "Change of character: from sideways to uptrend" (at the breakout above the range). `[P1 p19, p22]` Two post-ChoBev scenarios: **Scenario #1** ChoCh = "Consolidation"; **Scenario #2** ChoCh = "Test the high then decline."
- `[P3 p18]` "When a product begins CHoCH, it proves the CO has begun to step in."

### 2.6 Market structure (SMC vocabulary)

- **Bullish Market Structure** `[P2 p6]`: formed when price continuously makes **Higher Highs and Higher Lows**. When price breaks an old high and sets a new high, the trend is expected to continue. **New (higher) lows are the areas considered safe to trade** in an uptrend. Chart label **BMS** at each break of a prior high `[P2 p6–10]` (L14 15m, GEX 15m, MSB 30m, DIG 30m). The abbreviation is never expanded in text; from usage it marks a Break of Market Structure.
- **Bearish Market Structure** `[P2 p11]`: **Lower Highs and Lower Lows**. When price breaks an old low and sets a new low, the trend is expected to continue. **New (lower) highs are the areas considered safe to trade** in a downtrend. BMS marked at each break of a prior low `[P2 p11–14]` (CII 5m, CTI 15m, CTD 30m).

### 2.7 Liquidity / Stop hunt

- **Stop hunt / Hunt liquidity** `[P2 p22–23]`: "A phenomenon that occurs in markets with a high rate of leverage such as Crypto or Forex. It is usually created by the CO team to hunt liquidity. Specifically, the CO pushes price to the area where many traders place stop-losses and hunts those stop orders. This makes price spike up or down suddenly and then quickly reverse, becoming an opportunity for investors who recognise the situation."
- **Buy Stop Liquidity (BSL)** `[P2 p24]`: hunting the stop-losses of **Sell** orders. After BSL is taken, the market usually **reverses down** and the CO uses it to place sell orders. BSL usually sits at: PMH (Previous Month High), PWH (Previous Week High), PDH (Previous Day High), HOD (High Of Day), OLD High (Swing High), EQUAL HIGHS (Retail Resistance). Diagram: "X – PMH, PWH, PDH, HOD, OLD HIGH"; "BSL Taken" above X / above equal highs, followed by a down arrow.
- **Sell Stop Liquidity (SSL)** `[P2 p29]`: hunting the stop-losses of **Buy** orders. After SSL is taken, the market usually **reverses up** and the CO uses it to put buy orders into the market. SSL usually sits at: PML, PWL, PDL, LOD, OLD Low (Swing Low — the slide misprints "OLD High (Swing Low)"), EQUAL LOWS (Retail Support). Diagram: "X – PML, PWL, PDL, LOD, OLD LOW"; "SSL TAKEN" below X / below equal lows, followed by an up arrow.
- Examples `[P2 p25–28]` (TIG 30m, C4G 1h, CEN 1h, STB 30m): a wick above a range high labelled "stophunt", then decline. `[P2 p30–33]` (GEX 15m, MSB 30m, ITA 15m, VND 15m): a wick below a range low / equal lows labelled "stophunt", then rally; VND shows three boxed equal lows before the hunt.

### 2.8 Wyckoff accumulation events `[P1 p24]`

- **PS — Preliminary Support**: where significant buying begins to create a clearly stronger support zone after a long move down. Volume increases and spread widens, signalling the down move may be nearing its end.
- **SC — Selling Climax**: the point at which spread widens and volume increases significantly; selling pressure usually reaches its peak. Panic selling by the public here attracts large operators to buy at lower prices.
- **AR — Automatic Rally**: occurs because intense selling pressure has greatly diminished. A wave of buyers (mostly retail, or short-covering) easily pushes price up. The high of this rally helps define the **upper boundary of the accumulation TR**.
- **ST — Secondary Test**: re-tests supply/demand at the lower boundary of the TR, specifically at the SC. Because of the CO's large buying effort at SC, spread and volume usually **decrease** as price returns near SC. "The most common case is that the ST in Phase A is higher than SC; there are usually multiple STs in the whole structure."
- **Test**: the action of the CO checking supply throughout the TR. Usually appears right after a Spring/Shakeout. If supply is felt to be exhausted, the CO will consider pushing price up again.
- **Spring / Shakeout**: usually occurs **late in the TR** and lets strong hands perform a decisive test of remaining supply before the markup campaign begins. A **Spring** trades **below the lowest low of the TR and then reverses to close inside the TR**; this lets strong hands deceive the public about a continuing downtrend and buy more at a bargain. A **Shakeout** is the action where the CO deliberately makes retail think price will go down, with **most of the Shakeout candles closing below the lower boundary of the TR**, making retail think it is a SOW so they give up holdings and the CO accumulates enough to push price up. **Note: not every structure has a Spring or Shakeout.**
- **SOS — Sign of Strength**: the expression of strength of the whole structure when price has a **period of commitment above the highest high of the TR**, showing spread and volume rising steadily. Note: observe when price is in the upper half of the TR. Charts use **MSOS[D]** = major SOS in Phase D `[P1 p26–30]`; **UA[B]** = upthrust action in Phase B, "UA[B] as minor SOS" `[P1 p30]`, and a UA[B] can leave "a large number of buyers trapped" `[P1 p28]`.
- **LPS — Last Point of Support**: the pullback of price after creating an SOS, after growth from a Spring/Shakeout. **Two types: one formed after the SOS and one formed inside the TR.** Charts also label "LPS AREA [C]" `[P1 p29]`, "LPS[C]" `[P1 p30]` and "LPS AREA [D]" `[P1 p28, P3 p13]`.
- **BU — thorough absorption of supply (Back-Up)**: the phenomenon of absorbing all the supply sitting on the TR wall after creating an SOS; depending on how much supply remains, the BU takes the form of **one LPS or one structure**. Charts label "BU/LPS[D]". `[P3 p15]` schematic also uses **JAC** (jump across creek) and **BUEC** (back-up to edge of creek) between Spring and SOS, and shows that the SOS high becomes the **AR** and the subsequent reaction the **ST** of the next (re-accumulation) range.

### 2.9 Wyckoff accumulation phases `[P1 p25]`

- **Phase A** — "Marks the stopping of the previous downtrend." Until now supply dominated; approaching supply exhaustion is evidenced by PS and SC, usually very clear with widening spread and large volume describing the transfer of a large quantity from the public to strong hands. Once the intense selling is relieved, an AR follows (institutional demand plus short covering). A successful ST in the SC area shows less supply than before, with narrowing spread and decreasing volume. **If the ST goes lower than the SC, one may expect new lows or an extended consolidation.** The lows of SC and ST and the high of AR set the boundaries of the TR.
- **Phase B** — "Building the cause" (Law #2) for a new uptrend. Large institutions accumulate relatively low-priced product ahead of the next rise. Accumulation can take a long time and involves buying at lower prices and testing supply. Usually multiple STs in Phase B, plus minor SOS-type actions at the top of the TR. Institutions are the largest buyers as the TR develops, aiming to get as much as possible. When supply appears exhausted, the product is ready for Phase C.
- **Phase C** — Price undergoes a "test of remaining supply", allowing strong hands to determine whether outside supply is exhausted. A Spring moves below TR support (set in A and B), quickly reverses and moves back into the TR — a bear trap because the drop below support seems to signal continuation. A successful supply test represented by a Spring (or Shakeout) gives a **high-probability trading opportunity**. **A low-volume Spring indicates the product is likely ready to rise, so this is a good time to start opening a partial position.**
- **Phase D** — If the Phase C test is correct, what follows shows "the dominance of demand over supply", evidenced by a major SOS (widening spread, rising volume) and reactions (LPS) with declining supply volume. In Phase D price will move **at least to the upper boundary of the TR**. **LPS in this phase are usually the best places to add to profitable long positions.**
- **Phase E** — Price leaves the TR; "supply is completely controlled" and the rise is clearly strong. Failures such as shakeouts and typical down reactions are usually short-lived. New TRs (re-accumulation) include CO profit-taking and buying more.

### 2.10 Wyckoff distribution events `[P3 p2, p4]`

Terminology table `[P3 p4]`: PSY = Preliminary supply; BCLX = Buying climax; AR = Auto rally; ST = Secondary test; UT = Upthrust; UTAD = Upthrust after Distribution; SOW = Sign of weakness; LPSY = Last point of supply.

- **PSY — Preliminary Supply**: where the CO begins selling after a strong up move with volume and widening spread, signalling a trend change may be near.
- **BCLX — Buying Climax**: usually a clear increase in volume and spread. Buying peaks; the public rushes in to buy; the market is flooded with good news. Operators exploit it to sell their product.
- **AR — Automatic Reaction**: with intense buying greatly diminished after BCLX plus CO profit-taking and retail selling, the **low of this sell-off defines the lower boundary of the distribution TR**.
- **ST — Secondary Test**: price returns to the BCLX area to test supply/demand balance. For the next top to be confirmed, **supply must exceed demand** for the CO to hold price within the TR, so volume and spread **decrease** as price approaches BCLX resistance, forming **ST[A]**. **ST[B]** may take another form, a **UT (Upthrust)**, in which price moves above BCLX resistance and quickly reverses to close below it. **After a UT, price usually tests the lower boundary of the TR.**
- **UTAD — Upthrust After Distribution**: the main bull trap of distribution; a signal that the SOS has failed, going into distribution. It is like a Spring/Shakeout at the lower boundary of an accumulation TR. It creates an up move aimed at testing demand at the upper boundary of the distribution TR, and supply begins to increase more strongly. Like Spring/Shakeout, **UTAD is not a mandatory structural component** — Schematic #1 contains a UTAD, Schematic #2 does not (in #2 Phase C is an LPSY at resistance).
- **LPSY — Last Point of Supply**: after testing local resistance at the lower boundary of the TR (source wording), a **feeble rally on narrow spread** shows the market has significant difficulty rising. Inability to rise may be due to weak demand vs significant supply, or strong demand vs stronger supply. LPSY shows demand exhaustion and the CO's last sales before the decline becomes stronger.
- **SOW — Sign of Weakness**: observable when price moves down to the lower boundary of the TR, usually with a widening down spread and increasing volume. **Commitment below the initial AR support shows a change of character: supply now dominates.** Charts label **MSOW[D]** = major SOW in Phase D `[P3 p5–10]`; schematic also labels "SOW Phase B" for a Phase B test of the lower boundary `[P3 p1–3]`.

### 2.11 Wyckoff distribution phases `[P3 p3]`

- **Phase A** — "Stopping of the previous uptrend." Until now demand dominated; the first sign of supply entering is PSY and BCLX, usually followed by AR and an ST of BCLX, usually on declining volume. **However, the uptrend can also end without climactic action**, showing instead demand exhaustion with declining spread and volume — "shortening of thrust" on each rise — before significant supply appears.
- **Phase B** — "Building the cause" for a new downtrend. The CO sells large quantities preparing for the next decline. Same points as accumulation Phase B, except strong hands sell portions as the TR develops, aiming to exhaust as much remaining demand as possible. This leaves a footprint that the supply/demand balance tilts to supply.
- **Phase C** — May form as a UT or UTAD. UT/UTAD is the inverse of a Spring: at TR resistance it quickly reverses and closes inside the TR. It is the "test of remaining demand", a bull trap seeming to signal resumption of the uptrend but actually a false breakout to deceive traders. UT/UTAD lets the CO sell more at high prices to retail buying the breakout. Institutions following strong-hand footprints may want to **start short positions after a UT/UTAD; risk/reward is usually very large. However, strong hands create UTs to repeatedly stop traders from initiating shorts, so it is usually safer to wait for Phase D and LPSY confirmation.**
- **Phase D** — After the Phase C tests show us the last demand. Price commits below TR support. Evidence "supply is clearly dominant": spread and volume increase with a clear break of support, or a decline below the middle of the TR after a UT/UTAD. Usually multiple weak rallies in Phase D; **these LPSYs are excellent opportunities to initiate or add to short positions. Anyone still holding a long-term long in Phase D should exit quickly.**
- **Phase E** — Officially in the downtrend; "price leaves the TR" and supply controls the market. Once TR support is broken by a **major SOW**, the break is usually tested by an unsuccessful rally near support. Subsequent rallies in the decline are usually feeble. After a strong down move, climactic selling action can signal the start of a re-distribution or accumulation TR.

### 2.12 Volume character of distribution vs accumulation `[P3 p5]`

"During distribution, high or abnormal volume can be identified during development. Distribution ranges develop completely differently. A key characteristic is that they tend to develop **much faster than accumulation**, because it shows in volume during distribution. Compared with accumulation: **accumulation volume tends to decrease from Phase C onward, while distribution volume is larger and faster.**" Schematic shows highlighted volume spikes at BCLX, ST A, ST B, ST B, UTAD, LPSY (green arrow across the range = persistently elevated volume), then rising bars at MSOW/LPSY.

### 2.13 Local accumulation as Spring `[P3 p12]`

Schematic: PS → SC → AR (resistance line) → ST → Phase B → Spring (below SC support). Zoom-in on the Spring zone: it is itself a small accumulation with Phase A (PS, SC, AR, ST), Phase B (with a "UT Phase B"), Phase C — captioned "Local accumulation as Spring?" i.e. a Spring on the higher timeframe can be a complete lower-timeframe accumulation.

### 2.14 Absorption of supply `[P3 p17]`

Two types (PNJ 1D 2011–2018 example):
1. **Horizontal absorption (Hấp thụ ngang)** = absorption through consolidation, "accumulating" price. **No sign of extreme demand; supply gradually decreases.** Volume notation: `S<, D>` or `D<, D>S`. Chart volume annotations along the base: "high supply" → "supply decreasing" → `D>>` (breakout) → `S<<, D<<` (quiet).
2. **Vertical absorption (Hấp thụ dọc)** = absorption on the way up, in the "uptrend": **supply increases creating new (local) lows, demand increases significantly. Price rises a lot and quickly when signs of increased demand appear.** "This shows the negation of the subsequent reaction." Notation: `S>>, D>>>, D>S, S<`. Chart: circled pullback consolidations on the way up with `S>, D>` and rising volume arrows.
- Related chart labels: "HẤP THỤ" (absorption) at a low-volume Spring area followed by `S<<, D>>` `[P1 p27]`; "S<<, HẤP THỤ NGANG" (horizontal absorption) across the LPS AREA [C] `[P1 p29]`; "HẤP THỤ" on a large-volume bar at UTAD in distribution (supply absorbing demand) `[P3 p8]`.

### 2.15 Urgent Demand `[P3 p18]`

"When a product begins CHoCH, it proves the CO has begun to step in. Price moves from the Spring/Shakeout zone; we see signs of supply weakening, absorption on the way down. Usually, absorption at a slow speed of movement is a **wave or Trading Range**; **urgent demand** is where institutions hurriedly buy strongly in this product, making demand spike suddenly, **negating all previous supply**." Example DCM weekly: "Urgent Demand" at the 2020 low with `D>>>` and a rising volume arrow; VNINDEX March 2020 also marked "Urgent Demand" `[P1 p11]`.

### 2.16 Volume shorthand used on all charts

`S` = supply, `D` = demand; `<`/`>` repeated = relative magnitude (e.g. `S<<` small supply, `D>>>` very large demand). Combined states seen: `S<<, D>>` (accumulation/markup start), `S>>, D>>` (climactic — both sides heavy, at SC or BCLX), `S>>, D<<` (distribution), `S>>, D>>>` (urgent demand), `S<, D>` / `D<, D>S` (horizontal absorption), `S>>, D>>>, D>S, S<` (vertical absorption), `S<<<` (supply drying up in Phase B), `D>>` (SOS breakout). Other chart words: "KIỆT SỨC" = exhaustion (declining volume into the top) `[P3 p8]`; "THOÁT HÀNG" = unloading at UTAD `[P3 p8]`; "RŨ BỎ" = shake-off `[P1 p26]`; "Bẫy tăng giá" = bull trap `[P1 p26, P3 p7]`; "Selling climax" `[P1 p21]`; "Short" `[P1 p23]`.

### 2.17 Idealized entry / exit / stop-loss map `[P3 p19]`

Legend: solid green = **Stop-loss**; red dashed = **Commitment** (the level price must commit beyond to confirm). Accumulation: **Long-term Strategy Buy Zone** — POE#1 (at the Spring low, Sl#1 below it), POE#2 (first pullback after the break of the TR top, Sl#2). **Long-term Strategy Add-on / Swing Strategy Buy Zone** — POE#3, POE#4 (pullbacks in early markup, Sl#3, Sl#4 each raised under the prior swing low). Stop-losses step up under each successive higher low (trailing).

## 3. Actionable rules / checklists (IF/THEN, with citations)

### 3.1 Law-level rules
- R1 `[P1 p3]`: IF demand > supply THEN expect price up; IF supply > demand THEN expect price down; IF balanced THEN expect sideways.
- R2 `[P1 p4]`: IF a larger cause (wider/longer TR, larger P&F count) has been built THEN set a proportionally larger target; IF small cause THEN small target. (P&F counts: conservative / probable / aggressive.)
- R3 `[P1 p5]`: IF volume increases THEN treat as a sign of large-operator participation. Price moves without volume are not sustainable.
- R4 `[P1 p5–8]` Effort/result: IF a rising bar has large volume AND the next bars continue up THEN harmony (bullish). IF a rising bar has large volume AND price does NOT continue (reverses) THEN divergence (bearish). IF up-thrust bars widen with rising volume THEN harmony; IF up-thrust widens with declining volume THEN divergence. IF a down reversal comes with declining volume THEN harmony (benign); IF a down reversal comes with rising volume THEN divergence (supply). IF up-thrust with rising range and volume BREAKS resistance THEN harmony; IF same effort FAILS to break resistance THEN divergence (rejection).

### 3.2 Structure / auction rules
- R5 `[P2 p6]`: IF price prints HH + HL AND breaks the prior high (BMS) THEN trend expected to continue; the new higher low is the safe area to trade (buy).
- R6 `[P2 p11]`: IF price prints LH + LL AND breaks the prior low (BMS) THEN downtrend expected to continue; the new lower high is the safe area to trade (sell).
- R7 `[P2 p21]`: IF price stays in a new area for a period and goes sideways THEN acceptance (value moved). IF price returns quickly to the old value zone THEN rejection.
- R8 `[P2 p21]`: IF buyers and sellers stop agreeing inside a balance zone THEN the balance ends → imbalance (trend). IF price reaches a level where both sides agree again THEN the imbalance ends → new balance.
- R9 `[P2 p15–17]`: IF in a balance/range THEN that is the "efficient time" (negotiation); IF in an impulse THEN "inefficient time" (one side in control). Charts mark the range lows/consolidations as "efficient time" and the impulsive legs as "inefficient time".

### 3.3 Liquidity rules
- R10 `[P2 p24]`: IF price spikes above PMH/PWH/PDH/HOD/old swing high/equal highs AND quickly reverses (BSL taken) THEN expect reversal down; CO places sells there.
- R11 `[P2 p29]`: IF price spikes below PML/PWL/PDL/LOD/old swing low/equal lows AND quickly reverses (SSL taken) THEN expect reversal up; CO places buys there.
- R12 `[P2 p22–23]`: A stop hunt is recognised by a sudden move to the stop cluster followed by a quick reversal — that reversal is the opportunity.

### 3.4 Accumulation rules
- R13 `[P1 p25]`: IF PS and SC appear (widening spread, large volume) AND an AR follows THEN mark TR boundaries: lows of SC/ST = support, high of AR = resistance.
- R14 `[P1 p24–25]`: IF the ST returns toward SC with narrowing spread and lower volume THEN supply is less than before (constructive). IF the ST breaks below SC THEN expect new lows or extended consolidation (do not assume accumulation is complete).
- R15 `[P1 p24]`: IF price trades below the TR low and closes back inside THEN Spring. IF most candles close below the TR low before recovering THEN Shakeout. Do NOT require a Spring/Shakeout — not every structure has one.
- R16 `[P1 p25]`: IF a Spring/Shakeout occurs on LOW volume THEN the product is likely ready to rise → start a **partial** position.
- R17 `[P1 p24]`: IF a Test after the Spring shows supply exhausted (low volume, holds above the Spring low) THEN CO will consider pushing price up.
- R18 `[P1 p24]`: SOS requires price to **commit for a period above the TR high** with spread and volume rising steadily; look for it when price is already in the upper half of the TR.
- R19 `[P1 p25]`: IF SOS confirmed AND a pullback (LPS) forms on declining supply volume THEN LPS in Phase D is the best place to add to profitable longs. Expect Phase D to reach at least the TR upper boundary.
- R20 `[P1 p24]`: After SOS, expect a BU that absorbs the supply at the TR wall; IF much supply remains THEN BU forms a structure, IF little THEN BU is a single LPS.
- R21 `[P1 p25]`: IF price has left the TR (Phase E) THEN treat shakeouts/reactions as short-lived; new TRs are re-accumulation (CO takes profit and buys more).
- R22 `[P3 p12]`: IF a Spring zone on the higher timeframe shows PS/SC/AR/ST/UT structure on the lower timeframe THEN read it as a local accumulation (confirming the Spring).
- R23 `[P3 p15]`: IF SOS forms THEN the SOS high acts as AR and the next reaction as ST of the following re-accumulation range.

### 3.5 Distribution rules
- R24 `[P3 p2–3]`: IF PSY (selling on a strong up move with volume) then BCLX (volume + spread climax, good news) then AR THEN mark distribution TR: AR low = support, BCLX = resistance.
- R25 `[P3 p2]`: IF ST returns to BCLX on **lower** volume and spread THEN top confirmed (supply > demand) = ST[A]. IF ST pushes above BCLX and closes back below THEN it is a UT (ST[B]) → expect a test of the TR lower boundary next.
- R26 `[P3 p3]`: Distribution can begin WITHOUT a climax: IF each rally shows shortening thrust with declining spread and volume THEN treat as demand exhaustion (Phase A).
- R27 `[P3 p3]`: IF UT/UTAD (break above resistance, quick reversal, close inside TR) THEN it is the test of remaining demand / bull trap. Shorting after UT/UTAD has very large R/R BUT strong hands repeat UTs to stop shorts → **safer to wait for Phase D and an LPSY confirmation**.
- R28 `[P3 p2]`: LPSY = feeble narrow-spread rally after testing the lower boundary; IF such a rally fails to reach prior levels THEN demand is exhausted (CO's last sales).
- R29 `[P3 p2]`: IF price commits below the AR support with widening down spread and rising volume THEN SOW / change of character — supply dominates.
- R30 `[P3 p3]`: IF Phase D (commitment below TR support, or drop below the TR midpoint after UT/UTAD) THEN LPSYs are the places to initiate/add shorts; **exit any remaining long-term longs quickly**.
- R31 `[P3 p3]`: IF TR support breaks on a major SOW THEN expect an unsuccessful rally back toward support (test), then feeble rallies in the decline. IF climactic selling appears after the decline THEN watch for re-distribution or accumulation.
- R32 `[P3 p5]`: IF the range shows high/abnormal, persistent volume and develops fast THEN lean distribution; IF volume dries up from Phase C onward THEN lean accumulation.

### 3.6 ChoBev / ChoCh and absorption rules
- R33 `[P1 p17]`: IF the first counter-trend reaction shows rising spread and rising effort against the trend THEN ChoBev (early warning). IF the largest counter-trend reaction is followed by non-continuation of the trend THEN ChoCh (structure changed).
- R34 `[P1 p19, p22]`: After a bearish ChoBev in an uptrend (largest down reaction, wider spread, supply rising) THEN expect one of two ChoCh scenarios: (#1) consolidation, or (#2) test of the high then decline.
- R35 `[P3 p18]`: IF ChoCH appears at a Spring/Shakeout zone AND demand spikes suddenly negating prior supply (D>>>) THEN Urgent Demand — CO has stepped in; expect fast markup.
- R36 `[P3 p17]`: IF a base shows supply decreasing with no extreme demand (S<, D>) THEN horizontal absorption (accumulation base). IF pullbacks on the way up show supply creating local lows but demand overwhelming (S>>, D>>>, D>S) and price rises quickly THEN vertical absorption — the next reaction is "negated" (continuation).

### 3.7 Position-management map `[P3 p19]`
- R37: Long-term buys: POE#1 at the Spring (stop below Spring low), POE#2 at the first pullback after breaking the TR top (stop below that low). Add-on / swing buys at POE#3–4 on subsequent higher lows; each new stop under the newest higher low.
- R38: In re-accumulation: take swing profits / long-term partial profit at the range highs (Exit#1–3); re-buy at POE#1–4 with stops Sl#5–8 under the range lows.
- R39: In distribution: swing sell / long-term profit-taking at the tops (Exit#1, Exit#2); Exit#3 at the failed rally (stop Sl#9); Exit#4 = catastrophic exit on the last lower high before markdown.

## 4. Patterns with identification criteria

### 4.1 Effort-vs-result matrix `[P1 p5–8]`
| Pattern | Must be true | Must NOT be true | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harmony (continuation) | Large-volume up bar → subsequent bars continue up | Reversal on the next bars | Demand in control |
| Divergence (no result) | Large-volume up bar → price fails to continue / reverses | Continuation | Supply absorbing demand (bearish) |
| Harmony (thrust) | Widening up bars + rising volume | Volume declining while range widens | Healthy markup |
| Divergence (thrust) | Widening up bars + **declining** volume | — | Weak markup, likely reversal |
| Harmony (reversal) | Down reversal on **declining** volume | Rising volume on the decline | Pullback, not distribution |
| Divergence (reversal) | Down reversal on **rising** volume | — | Supply entering |
| Harmony (breakout) | Rising range + rising volume AND close above resistance | Close back below | Genuine break |
| Divergence (breakout) | Rising range + rising volume BUT no close above resistance; large red bar back into range | Acceptance above | Rejection / upthrust |
| Small-range + large-volume bar | Small body with the largest volume bar | — | Divergence ("Phân kỳ") – absorption at that price |

### 4.2 Accumulation range (Phases A–E) `[P1 p24–30]`
- Must: prior downtrend; PS then SC (widest spread, highest volume — "S>>, D>>" on charts), AR, ST on lower volume; Phase B with drying supply ("S<<" / "S<<<"); Phase C Spring/Shakeout (close back inside range; low volume preferred) or an LPS area at support; Phase D SOS/MSOS breakout with "D>>" and LPS/BU pullbacks on low supply; price commits above the range high.
- Must NOT: ST breaking below SC with follow-through (→ not yet accumulation); persistent high volume in the range from Phase C on (→ distribution character); SOS without a period of commitment above the TR high.
- Chart labels to expect: PS, SC, AR, ST[A], ST[B], UA[B], SPRING[C], Test, LPS[C]/LPS AREA[C], LPS[D]/LPS AREA[D], BU/LPS[D], MSOS[D]; volume: S>>,D>> at SC; S<< in B; D>> at MSOS.

### 4.3 Spring vs Shakeout `[P1 p24]`
- Spring: single excursion below the TR low that closes back inside the TR.
- Shakeout: most candles close below the lower boundary (looks like a SOW to retail) before recovery.
- Neither is mandatory. A low-volume Spring = higher probability. A Test after it should hold on low volume.

### 4.4 Distribution range (Phases A–E) `[P3 p2–10]`
- Must: prior uptrend; PSY, BCLX (volume + spread climax, "S>>,D>>" on charts), AR (low = support), ST[A] on lower volume near BCLX or ST[B] as UT above BCLX closing back inside; Phase B tests on both sides; Phase C UTAD (or LPSY at resistance if no UTAD); Phase D LPSY rallies (narrow, feeble) then MSOW commitment below the AR support with rising volume/spread; Phase E test of the broken support fails.
- Must NOT: SOS-type commitment above the TR high with steady volume (that is accumulation/UA); volume drying up into Phase C (accumulation character).
- Chart labels: PSY, BCLX, AR, ST[A], ST[B], UT[B], UTAD[C], LPSY[C], LPSY[D], MSOW[D]; volume: S>> arc across the range, D<< at the UTAD, S>> on breakdown; "KIỆT SỨC" (exhaustion) as volume declines into the top, "HẤP THỤ" at UTAD, "THOÁT HÀNG" (unloading).

### 4.5 UT / UTAD vs SOS
- UT/UTAD: break above resistance, **quick** reversal, close inside the TR, supply increases afterwards `[P3 p2–3]`.
- SOS: **period of commitment** above the TR high, spread + volume rising steadily `[P1 p24]`.
- Distinguishing test: acceptance (time above, sideways) vs rejection (fast return) `[P2 p21]`.

### 4.6 ChoBev → ChoCh sequence `[P1 p17–23]`
- ChoBev: first counter-trend reaction with (1) largest reaction so far against the trend, (2) increased spread, (3) increased opposing effort/supply.
- ChoCh: the largest reaction against the trend AND no continuation → structure flips (downtrend→sideways at Phase A; sideways→uptrend at Phase D; uptrend→sideways at distribution Phase A).
- Follow-ups: "consolidation" or "test high then decline".

### 4.7 Market-structure break (BMS) `[P2 p6–14]`
- Bullish: sequence of HH/HL; BMS = close above the prior swing high; entry area = the new higher low.
- Bearish: sequence of LH/LL; BMS = close below the prior swing low; entry area = the new lower high.

### 4.8 Stop hunt (BSL/SSL) `[P2 p22–33]`
- Must: an obvious stop cluster level (PMH/PWH/PDH/HOD/old high/equal highs, or the low-side mirror); a sudden wick/spike through it; a quick reversal back inside.
- Must NOT: acceptance beyond the level (then it is a true break, not a hunt).
- Direction: BSL taken → down; SSL taken → up.

### 4.9 Horizontal vs vertical absorption `[P3 p17]`
- Horizontal: sideways base; supply steadily decreasing; no extreme demand; S<, D> (or D<, D>S). Ends with D>> breakout.
- Vertical: pullback consolidations inside an uptrend; supply creates local lows but demand dwarfs it (S>>, D>>>, D>S, S<); price resumes up quickly.

### 4.10 Urgent Demand `[P3 p18, P1 p11]`
- Must: a ChoCH at/after a Spring/Shakeout; a sudden demand spike (D>>>, volume surge) that negates all prior supply; price leaves the low quickly (no slow wave/TR absorption).

### 4.11 Local accumulation as Spring `[P3 p12]`
- Must: higher-timeframe Spring below the TR low; on the lower timeframe the excursion shows PS–SC–AR–ST (Phase A), Phase B with a UT, Phase C — a complete mini accumulation.

### 4.12 Auction-theory cycle `[P2 p3, p15–21]`
- Balance box (accumulation, "efficient time") → fast price move ("inefficient time") → "building pre-tension" (small mid-trend box) → trend continuation → new balance box.

## 6. Conflicts or ambiguities inside these sources

1. **"3 laws" vs "4 theories"** — `[P1 p2]` lists four; the slide headers `[P1 p3–5, p10]` say "3 Wyckoff laws". Auction theory is the fourth and is developed in PDF 2.
2. **Spring vs Shakeout** — `[P1 p24]` gives a distinguishing rule (Shakeout = most candles close below the TR low) but everywhere else uses "Spring/Shakeout" as a pair; VIC/ACB charts label "Shakeout [C]" where a single excursion is visible.
3. **ST relative to SC** — text says "most common case: ST in Phase A is higher than SC" and "if ST goes lower than SC expect new lows or extended consolidation" `[P1 p24–25]`, yet several examples (CTG 30m `[P1 p27]`, VDS `[P1 p29]`, ACB `[P1 p26]`) draw ST[A]/ST[B] at or marginally below the SC line while still labelling the range accumulation.
4. **LPS in Phase C** — `[P1 p24]` says LPS forms after SOS or inside the TR; charts additionally label "LPS AREA [C]" / "LPS[C]" `[P1 p29–30]` as a Phase C alternative to a Spring. The text never defines an LPS-based Phase C.
5. **Shorting UT/UTAD** — `[P3 p3]` simultaneously says institutions "may start shorts after UT/UTAD, R/R usually very large" and "usually safer to wait for Phase D and LPSY confirmation". Treat the former as aggressive, the latter as the recommended default.
6. **Stop hunt scope** — `[P2 p22]` says stop hunts occur "in high-leverage markets such as Crypto or Forex", but all eight examples `[P2 p25–33]` are Vietnamese cash equities (and the term is applied there).
7. **SSL level typo** — `[P2 p29]` lists "OLD High (Swing Low)"; the diagram says "OLD LOW". Read as Old Low.
8. **LPSY definition wording** — `[P3 p2]` says LPSY comes "after testing local resistance at the **lower** boundary of the TR"; the schematics show LPSY as a lower high after a SOW toward the lower boundary. The phrase is likely "after testing support at the lower boundary".
9. **MSOS / MSOW / UA / JAC / BUEC / BMS never defined in text** — they appear only as chart labels. Inferred from context: MSOS = major Sign of Strength (Phase D), MSOW = major Sign of Weakness (Phase D; text `[P3 p3]` writes "MAJROR SOW"), UA[B] = upthrust action in Phase B (DIG chart calls it "minor SOS"), JAC = jump across creek, BUEC = back-up to edge of creek, BMS = break of market structure.
10. **Distribution volume claim vs examples** — `[P3 p5]` says distribution volume is "larger and faster", yet the VND example `[P3 p8]` annotates "KIỆT SỨC" (exhaustion = declining volume) through Phases A–B and the PNJ example `[P3 p6]` shows `D<<` at the UTAD. The rule applies to the range as a whole (S>> arc), not to every event.
11. **ChoCh (Wyckoff) vs BMS (SMC)** — PDF 1 uses ChoCh for trend→range and range→trend transitions; PDF 2 uses BMS for continuation breaks. The decks do not reconcile the two vocabularies; they are complementary, not synonyms.
12. **70/30 statistic** — `[P2 p2]` gives no source or measurement window.
13. **"Test" placement** — `[P1 p24]` says Test usually appears right after a Spring/Shakeout; the schematics also draw "Test" in Phase B/C after an ST and PDF 3 `[P3 p2]` labels "TEST" right after the UTAD in distribution.
14. **Cause & Effect targets** — the P&F slide `[P1 p4]` shows three counts but gives no rule for choosing between conservative / probable / aggressive.
